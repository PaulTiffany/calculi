# 01 — Things Change

**Start with:** counting and simple multiplication.\
**By the end:** tell the difference between how fast something changes and how much has added up.

Before calculus has symbols, it has a question:

> **What is changing?**

A car moves. Water fills a glass. Soup cools. A plant grows.

## Two questions about one trip

| Instrument | Question | Example reading |
|---|---|---|
| Speedometer | How fast are we traveling now? | 40 miles per hour |
| Odometer | How much distance has added up? | 80 miles since we started |

The speedometer shows a **rate**: an amount per unit of time. The odometer shows a **total**.

Driving at a steady 40 miles per hour for two hours adds 80 miles. Each hour adds another 40.

The words after the numbers matter. **Miles per hour** and **miles** answer different questions.

## Try it

After those two hours, you stop for lunch. What happens to the speed reading? What happens to the distance already traveled?

<details>
<summary>Check your reasoning</summary>

The speed becomes zero. The trip distance remains 80 miles. Stopping does not erase the journey.

A rate can be zero while the total is large.

</details>

## Try it somewhere else

A tap puts 3 liters of water into an empty bucket each minute. It runs steadily for 4 minutes. No water spills or drains away.

Which number is a rate? How much water is in the bucket when the tap stops?

<details>
<summary>Check and connect</summary>

The rate is **3 liters per minute**. Four groups of 3 liters make **12 liters**.

Water takes the place of distance. Liters per minute takes the place of miles per hour. The same reasoning works because a steady rate builds up over time.

</details>

## Say it in your own words

Name a rate and a total from your day. Include their units. A useful answer explains both **how fast** and **how much**.

> Ordinary calculus helps us understand change and how change adds up.

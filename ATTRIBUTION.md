# Attribution and Source Provenance

This repository is a pedagogical synthesis. Its purpose is to teach calculi faithfully while making historical lineage, formal dependence, and original interpretation visible.

The original explanatory text in this repository is licensed under [CC BY 4.0](LICENSE.md). **Source materials are not relicensed.**

## Attribution principles

Every substantive lesson should make clear:

1. **Who originated or developed the calculus being taught.**
2. **Which primary or near-primary sources the lesson relies on.**
3. **Which claims are historical, formal, interpretive, or original to this atlas.**
4. **Where a modern correction, dispute, or refinement changes the received story.**
5. **When a bridge between calculi is only a structural analogy or conjecture.**

A bibliography entry alone is not enough when provenance matters to understanding the lesson.

## How to mark source-dependent passages

Use one or more of the following, as appropriate:

- **Primary source:** for definitions, syntax, theorems, historical introductions, or author-stated motivations.
- **Later correction/refinement:** when newer scholarship changes an earlier claim.
- **Pedagogical reconstruction:** for toy examples or simplified notation created for teaching.
- **Structural analogy:** for a comparison not asserted by the source authors.
- **Conjectured bridge:** for a proposed formal relationship that remains to be proved.

## Quotation discipline

Prefer paraphrase and formal citation over long quotation.

When exact wording matters, quote minimally and identify the source immediately. Do not copy substantial portions of source text into this repository.

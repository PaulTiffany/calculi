# 09 — Frontier: Goertzel's d-Calculus

## Why this page is deliberately incomplete

Ben Goertzel publicly mentioned a new “distinction calculus” / “d-calculus” in September 2026.

As of **2026-09-22**, this repository has not identified a public formal specification containing enough primitives, syntax, semantics, and laws to teach the calculus faithfully.

So we do **not** reverse-engineer it from teasers.

That restraint is part of the pedagogy.

## What is public

Goertzel has publicly described d-calculus as a new mathematical direction developed in connection with AI mathematical conjecturing.

His earlier 2019 distinction-graph program provides a clear pre-existing research lineage centered on observer-relative distinguishability and Dynamic Distinction Graphs.

These facts make distinction graphs an obvious comparison point.

They do **not** establish that d-calculus is simply distinction graphs with new notation.

## Questions to answer when the formalism appears

### Primitive

What comes first?

- observer?
- distinction?
- record?
- process?
- transformation?
- boundary?

### Syntax

What are valid expressions?

### Dynamics

What is the fundamental rewrite / composition law?

### Identity

When do two distinction structures count as equivalent?

### Fork and merge

If an agent forks into two continuations, how are inherited and divergent distinctions represented?

If branches merge, what is preserved or lost?

### Observer

Is observerhood assumed, indexed, reconstructed, or emergent?

### Loss

Does the calculus have an explicit notion analogous to quotient, residue, contextuality, or irrecoverability?

### Relation to prior work

How does it relate formally to distinction graphs / DDGs, calculus of indications, process calculi, and current recursive-self-improvement safety constructions?

## Comparison protocol

When public definitions arrive:

1. reconstruct the native object first;
2. quote the formal primitives accurately;
3. add primary references;
4. build the smallest working example;
5. populate the seven teaching questions;
6. construct translations only after the native object is understood;
7. mark every cross-calculus edge by relation type;
8. preserve differences instead of forcing convergence.

## What we will not do

We will not say:

- “d-calculus is Fuzzy Calculus”;
- “Fuzzy Calculus contains d-calculus”;
- “d-calculus supersedes Fuzzy Calculus”;
- “d-calculus derives from Principia Symbolica”;

unless formal evidence supports the statement.

The purpose of this atlas is to make future comparison easier, not to pre-decide it.

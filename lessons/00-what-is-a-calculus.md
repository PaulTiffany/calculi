# 00 — What Makes a Calculus Formal?

> Read the three [beginner pages](../start/01-things-change.md) first if calculus is new to you.

You already know the simple idea:

> **A calculus is a rulebook for working with a kind of change or transformation.**

This page adds a little more structure.

## Five parts of a calculus

Most calculi give us some version of these five things:

1. **Objects** — the things we work with.
2. **Starting ideas** — what the system assumes at the beginning.
3. **Moves** — what we are allowed to do.
4. **Rules** — when those moves are valid.
5. **Results** — how we decide when two outcomes count as the same or different.

## The same frame, different worlds

| Calculus | What it works with | Main kind of move |
|---|---|---|
| ordinary calculus | changing amounts | find change or total accumulation |
| λ-calculus | symbolic expressions | substitute and reduce |
| sequent calculus | proof statements | make a valid inference |
| π-calculus | communicating processes | send and receive names |
| calculus of indications | distinctions | mark and simplify |
| distinction graphs | what an observer can tell apart | connect indistinguishable items |
| Fuzzy Calculus | bounded observation | differentiate, integrate, and track residue |

## Why this matters

Two calculi can look similar without being the same.

They may both use arrows. They may both talk about change. One may even be able to represent the other.

But the important questions are:

- What does each calculus treat as an object?
- What move is basic?
- What counts as the same result?

Those answers tell us what kind of world the calculus was built to describe.

## One sentence to remember

> **A formal calculus tells us what exists in its world, what moves are allowed, and how to judge the results.**
